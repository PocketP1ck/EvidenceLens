# IL-17A inhibition (secukinumab) in Crohn's disease: evidence against benefit

**Question:** Would inhibiting IL-17A help patients with Crohn's disease?

**Assessment:** Opposed by retrieved evidence. The only randomized human evidence retrieved, a phase 2 secukinumab trial in moderate-to-severe Crohn's disease, showed no benefit and was stopped for futility. Secukinumab had more adverse events, including infections. Case reports of IBD onset or worsening on IL-17 inhibitors point the same way, but they cannot establish causality. [1][2][3]

## Key findings
- In a randomized phase 2 trial (n=59), secukinumab 10 mg/kg IV ×2 gave a CDAI change of -29.2 vs -63.1 with placebo at week 6. The trial was terminated for futility. [1] _(F1)_
- The publication concluded that IL-17A blockade was ineffective in Crohn's disease and that adverse events were more frequent than with placebo, including infections. This is the same study as the registration, not independent corroboration. [2] _(F2)_
- Secukinumab is well characterized as an IL-17A inhibitor and is approved at phase 4 for other immune diseases. Crohn's disease remains at max phase 2, and a secukinumab–IL-17A co-crystal structure exists (PDB 9SFX). [4][5] _(F5, F6)_

## Opposing evidence
- The randomized trial showed no efficacy, with numerically worse CDAI change and response rate on drug, and was stopped for futility. [1] _(F1)_
- The publication reports ineffectiveness plus excess infections (20 on secukinumab, none on placebo). [2] _(F2)_
- Case series describe new or worsening IBD on IL-17 inhibitors given for other diseases. This suggests IL-17A may have a protective role in the gut, but the evidence is anecdotal. [3] _(F3)_

## Context differences
- Open Targets gives a low overall association (0.1), with most of the signal from literature. This is a ranking, not a probability of success. [6] _(F4)_
- Secukinumab's approvals are in psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis. ChEMBL max phase is not efficacy in Crohn's disease, where the phase is 2. [4] _(F5)_
- The IBD case reports come from patients with psoriasis or spondyloarthritis, not from Crohn's trial populations. [3] _(F3)_

## Limitations
- Only one randomized Crohn's trial was found, and it was small and short with a single dose regimen.
- The placebo response was large and the primary analysis was Bayesian with historical placebo information.
- The case series evidence comes from conference abstracts without denominators.
- The trial tested one antibody (secukinumab). It does not exclude other ways of targeting the IL-17 pathway, such as IL-17A/F or the receptor, and it says nothing about subgroups.

## Unknowns
- Whether the lack of benefit reflects the biology (IL-17A protective in the gut barrier), too little drug at the mucosa, or patient selection.
- The results of the open-label extension NCT01009281 (n=7) were not examined.
- Whether any genetic subgroup, such as those explored in the paper, might respond.

## Next research questions
- What mechanistic and animal evidence shows IL-17A protects the intestinal epithelial barrier?
- Have later trials of IL-17 pathway inhibitors (brodalumab, ixekizumab) in IBD reported safety signals?
- How do IL-23/p19 inhibitors, which reduce IL-17 indirectly, succeed in Crohn's disease where direct IL-17A blockade failed?
